- Introduction
	- Forensics is the application of methods & procedures to investigate & solve crimes
	- Branch of forensics that investigates cyber crimes is known as digital forensics
	- Cyber crime is any criminal activity conducted on or by using a digital device
- Digital Forensics Methodology
	- Digital forensics teams can approach cases using a variety of tools/techniques but the NIST (National Institute of Standards & Technology) defines a general process to follow
		- NIST works on defining frameworks for different areas of technology
		- 4 Phases
			- **Collection:** Gather and preserve evidence without altering it.
			- **Examination:** Filter and extract relevant data from collected evidence.
			- **Analysis:** Correlate evidence to reconstruct relevant activities and draw conclusions.
			- **Reporting:** Document methods, findings, and recommendations for stakeholders.
		- Part of the collection phase requires various different tools, collection, and analysis methodologies dependent on the category of evidence
			- **Computer:** Investigates computers and their data.
			- **Mobile:** Investigates phones/tablets, including calls, messages, and GPS.
			- **Network:** Investigates network traffic and logs.
			- **Database:** Investigates database intrusions, modification, and data theft.
			- **Cloud:** Investigates data stored in cloud infrastructure.
			- **Email:** Investigates emails for phishing, fraud, and malicious campaigns.
- Evidence Aquisition
	- General practices must be followed while the evidence is acquired
		- Proper Authoization
			- Forensics team needs to obtain authorization from authorities before collecting data
			- Unauthorized data collection can be deemed inadmissable in court
		- Chain of Custody
			- Key details to include
				- Description of the evidence (name, type).
				- Name of individuals who collected the evidence.
				- Date and time of evidence collection.
				- Storage location of each piece of evidence.
				- Access times and the individual record who accessed the evidence.
			- Creates a proper trail of evidence and helps to preserve it
				- Proves integrity and reliability of the evidence in court
		- Use of Write Blockers
			- Important tool of digital forensics team
			- Ensures data integrity
- Windows Forensics
	- Background
		- Most common types of evidence collected from crime scenes are desktop computers and laptops
	- Windows OS Forensic Image Types
		- **Disk Image:** Non-volatile data stored on HDD/SSD, such as files and browsing history.
		- **Memory Image:** Volatile data in RAM, such as running processes, open files, and network connections. **Capture first** before shutdown/restart.
	- Tools used:
		- FTK Imager
		- Autopsy
		- Dumpit
		- Volatility
