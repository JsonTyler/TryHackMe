- AS02: Security Misconfigurations
	- What It Is
		- This happens when systems/servers/applications are deployed with unsafe defaults, incomplete settings, or exposed services
			- Creates easy entry points for attackers
	- Why It Matters
		- Even small misconfigurations can expose sensitive data, enable privilege escalation, or give attackers a way into the system
- AS03: Software Supply Chain Failures
	- What It Is
		- This happens when applications rely on components, libraries, services or models that are compromised/outdated/improperly verified
		- Attackers exploit these weak links to inject malicious code/bypass security/steal sensitive data
	- Why It Matters
		- Modern applications are built from many third-party packages, APIs, and models. One compromised dependency can compromise your entire system, allowing attackers to gain access without ever touching your own code. Supply chain attacks can be automated and distributed, making them hard to detect and very damaging.
- AS04: Cryptographic Failures
	- What It Is
		- Cryptographic failures happen when encryption is used incorrectly or not at all. This includes weak algorithms, hard-coded keys, poor key handling, or unencrypted sensitive data. These flaws let attackers access information that should be private.
	- Why It Matters
		- Web apps use cryptography to protect data in transit, at rest, and for authentication. **Weak or missing cryptography can expose sensitive data** such as passwords, tokens, and personal information. Attackers may exploit this through MITM attacks, brute-forcing weak keys, or exposed secrets.
- AS06: Insecure Design
	- What It Is
		- Insecure design happens when flawed logic or architecture is built into a system from the start. These flaws stem from skipped threat modelling, no design requirements or reviews, or accidental errors.
	- Why It Matters
		- You can't patch an insecure design. It's built into the workflow, logic, and trust boundaries. Fixing it means rethinking how systems, and now AI, make decisions.
	- Insecure Design In The AI Era
		- AI introduces new design risks. **Prompt injection** can let attackers manipulate the model by mixing malicious input with system instructions, potentially exposing hidden data. Relying too heavily on AI output can also create unsafe systems, so important decisions should still have human oversight. **Poisoned models** trained on untrusted or unsafe data can contain hidden behaviors or backdoors that compromise the system
